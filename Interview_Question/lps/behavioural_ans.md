# General Behavioral Questions
1.	Tell me about a time when your manager set reasonable demands. Follow up: Describe a situation with unreasonable demands.

**Situation:**
I was assigned to build a custom notifications feature where admins could send real-time notifications and also schedule them for future delivery.

**Task:**
I needed to design and implement this end-to-end, including API design, scheduling logic, and database interactions.

**Action:**
Initially, I estimated a reasonable timeline based on my understanding. However, during a discussion with my manager, he walked me through how our existing notification system worked, especially the database stored procedures and scheduling mechanism.

This helped me avoid reinventing parts of the system and reuse existing logic effectively. I revised my design accordingly and simplified parts of the implementation.

**Result:**
Because of that collaboration, I was able to reduce the development time by around 30% while still meeting all requirements. The feature was delivered efficiently and aligned well with existing system architecture.

**Learning:**
I learned that aligning early with experienced team members can significantly improve both design quality and delivery speed.

# Follow up: Describe a situation with unreasonable demands.
**Situation:**
I was working on a quiz feature that required full-stack development — frontend UI, backend APIs, and database stored procedures.

**Task:**
I estimated around 25 days to complete the feature based on the scope and the fact that I was handling all layers independently.

**Action:**
My manager initially felt the estimate was too high. Instead of pushing back immediately, I scheduled time with him and walked through a detailed breakdown of the work — including number of APIs, UI components, database procedures, and integration points.

I explained the complexity and dependencies involved in each part, which made the effort more transparent.

**Result:**
After reviewing the breakdown, he agreed that the estimate was reasonable. This alignment helped avoid unrealistic pressure and ensured we could deliver the feature with proper quality.

**Learning:**
I learned the importance of communicating estimates with clear breakdowns. What may seem unreasonable at a high level often becomes understandable when you explain the details.


# 2.	Tell me about one of the biggest accomplishments in your career so far.
**Situation:**
Early in my career at Bajaj Electricals, I was assigned responsibility for a regional block covering around 190–200 villages. At the time, the region was running at a loss, and the documentation and reporting quality were poor. The team’s credibility was also low due to inconsistent progress updates.

**Task:**
My goal was to stabilize operations, improve reporting accuracy, and turn the region into a profitable unit.

**Action:**
I started by visiting most of the villages along with my supervisors to understand the ground reality instead of relying only on reports. I emphasized to the team the importance of accurate reporting, even if progress was slower than expected, because rebuilding trust was critical.

I worked closely with the field staff to identify gaps in execution, improve coordination, and ensure consistent tracking of work. Over time, we focused on disciplined execution, better communication, and accountability within the team.

**Result:**
After around 8 months of sustained effort, the region transitioned from being loss-making to becoming one of the top profit-contributing blocks.

More importantly, we rebuilt trust within the team and improved the reliability of our reporting.

**Learning:**
This experience taught me that strong ownership, ground-level understanding, and building trust within a team can significantly turn around underperforming systems.

prioritization = impact + urgency + communication

**How I manage multiple priorities:**
When I have multiple priorities, I first try to understand the impact and urgency of each task. I typically break work into smaller units and align them based on deadlines, dependencies, and business impact.

For example, during my project work, I often had to balance development tasks, debugging issues, and sometimes urgent fixes. In such cases, I would first handle high-impact or blocking tasks, then schedule focused time for deeper development work.

I also try to communicate clearly with stakeholders if priorities conflict, so expectations are aligned and I can focus on the most important tasks.

---

**Preference for dynamic vs repetitive work:**
I prefer working in a dynamic environment with changing priorities. I find it engaging because it requires quick problem-solving and adaptability.

However, I also understand the importance of consistency in execution. So even in dynamic environments, I try to maintain a structured approach to ensure quality and timely delivery.

**Learning:**
This experience taught me the importance of setting clear goals, breaking them into manageable steps, and staying consistent even when progress feels slow.


# 6.	Describe a positive leadership or managerial style you liked from one of your previous managers. How did it influence your work style?
**Situation:**
In my first IT company, which was a startup, I worked under a manager whose leadership style I really appreciated, especially during high-pressure delivery timelines.

**Leadership Style:**
What stood out was how he handled situations when deadlines were approaching but it became clear that the work might not be completed on time despite the developer’s best efforts.

Instead of putting pressure on individuals, he would bring the entire team together and transparently explain the situation. He would then ask team members if they could temporarily support other projects by slowing down their own tasks and helping where needed.

He effectively followed a “divide and collaborate” approach, ensuring that the workload was distributed and the team worked together to meet commitments.

He also stayed connected with the team during late working hours, checking in on progress and supporting us when needed. After successful deliveries, he made sure to recognize everyone’s contribution and celebrate the effort.

---

**Impact on me:**
This influenced my work style in two major ways.

First, I learned the importance of collaboration over individual ownership when working under tight deadlines. I started proactively helping teammates when I saw blockers instead of focusing only on my tasks.

Second, I adopted the habit of clear communication — especially during delays — so that problems are visible early and can be solved collectively.

---

**Result:**
Because of this influence, I became more team-oriented and proactive in handling high-pressure situations, which has helped me work more effectively in collaborative environments.

9.	How do you prioritize and manage multiple tasks or projects? Provide an example of a time when you successfully juggled several tasks at once.
**How I prioritize and manage multiple tasks:**
When I have multiple tasks or projects, I usually prioritize them based on impact, urgency, and dependencies.

I first identify if any task is blocking others or has a direct business impact, and I handle those first. I also break larger tasks into smaller parts so I can track progress clearly and switch context efficiently when needed.

If priorities conflict, I communicate early with stakeholders to ensure alignment on what should be addressed first.

---

**Example:**
During my project work, I had to handle multiple responsibilities at the same time — including feature development, bug fixes, and sometimes urgent production-related issues.

In one instance, I was working on a feature while also receiving bug reports that were affecting users. I prioritized fixing the high-impact issues first to stabilize the system, and then resumed my feature development.

To manage both efficiently, I divided my time into focused blocks — handling urgent issues first and then dedicating uninterrupted time to development tasks. I also kept stakeholders informed about progress and any delays.

---

**Result:**
This approach helped me deliver both the fixes and the feature without major delays, while maintaining system stability.

---

**Learning:**
I learned that effective prioritization is not just about managing time, but about making the right decisions on what creates the most impact and communicating those decisions clearly.
